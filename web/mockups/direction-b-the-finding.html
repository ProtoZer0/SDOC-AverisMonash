Direction B

The Finding

The argument: a verdict is a statement, so say it first and say it large. The screen opens with the finding in one sentence, at display size, the way an inspector's report opens with its conclusion. Beneath it the seven details are seven full-width rows, instruction on the left, draft on the right, verdict word and shape leading each row. Click a row and an evidence band unfolds directly beneath it with the exact source lines, numbered, the value swept. The refusal uses the identical screen: the sentence changes to "Stopped", the draft column truthfully reads "not read", and a two-part panel says what was read and what was not. The refusal is not a different, smaller page; it is the same confident page with an honest gap.

What it costs. The draft stops looking like a bill of lading; it becomes a table of what the draft says. "We read To the Order of as the receiver" becomes a small caption under the field name rather than something you see on the page. Display type has to stay Archivo and must not creep into the mono values, or the semantic breaks. Opening several bands makes the list long.
