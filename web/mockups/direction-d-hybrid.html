Direction D · the hybrid of A and C

Facing Pages, with Margin Notes

The argument: evidence is a place, and every correction gets a note. From A: the instruction and the draft lie open side by side as the two real documents, the seven compared lines named in the margin, seven hairline threads stitching each to its twin, and a click that draws the thread and sweeps both values. From C: a red rule in the draft's gutter at every correction, the wrong words struck and the right words written in beneath, and a note in the right margin level with each mark that explains it in plain words. The note no longer needs to unfold the instruction line, because the instruction is already open on the left; its link selects the field instead, so the thread and the note point at the same evidence. Click a matched value and a quiet "checked" note appears level with it. The correction notes sit in a row beneath the pages, so the two documents keep the full width. Lines that are never compared are greyed, so the seven named lines read as the signal. The worklist takes A's counter strip and C's "next thing to send" column, and turns that column into a filter row.

What it costs. The notes are no longer level with their marks, so the red gutter rule and the thread have to do the anchoring. On a short laptop screen the notes start just below the fold. Below 1160px the pages stack and the threads switch off, as they already do in the app.
