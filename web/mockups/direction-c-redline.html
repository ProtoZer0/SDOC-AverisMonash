Direction C

Redline

The argument: the output is a marked-up draft you could send straight back to the carrier. One sheet only, the draft, as the real document, and the instruction appears on it the way a proofreader appears on a galley: a red rule in the gutter, the wrong words struck, the right words written in beneath, and a note in the margin level with each mark that explains the correction and points at its source. Click a mark, or its note, and the note unfolds to show the exact instruction line the correction came from, swept. Everything that matched gets its "checked" word on the page and nothing else, so the eye goes to the red rules. When there is no draft to mark up, the sheet becomes the reply you would send instead, drafted and not sent, listing the seven values we did read and stating exactly why the check stopped.

What it costs. The instruction is never shown whole, only line by line on request, so a judge who wants to see both documents has to ask. The margin notes are positioned to their lines by script and must be pushed apart when two corrections sit close together. The refusal page is the boldest of the three and needs the words "draft reply, not sent" to stay honest, because the product records the request and does not email anyone.
